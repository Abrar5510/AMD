## 1. Customer interview script (30 minutes)

**Who:** plant training managers, EHS (health and safety) managers, quality managers, maintenance leads. Not IT.

**Rules:** Ask about their past, not our future. No demo until the last 5 minutes. Write their exact words down. The goal is to learn, not to sell.

**Opening (2 min)**
> "We're building something for training new technicians on hands-on jobs. I'm not here to sell. I want to understand how you do it today. Is it OK if I take notes?"

**How they train today (10 min)**
1. Walk me through the last time a new technician learned a job like a lockout/tagout or a changeover. Who taught them? How long did it take?
2. How do you know when someone is ready to do it alone? Who decides, and what gets written down?
3. Where do your work instructions live today: paper, PDFs, a system? Who writes them, and how long does a new one take?
4. Which of your experts would you least like to lose? What happens to their knowledge if they leave?

**The pain (8 min)**
5. Tell me about the last time a new hire did a step wrong or skipped one. What did it cost: downtime, scrap, an injury, an audit finding?
6. How long until a new hire is fully productive? Has anyone measured it?
7. What have you already tried to fix this? Why did it stick, or not?
8. If training took half as long, what would that be worth to you in a year?

**Buying (5 min)**
9. Who would have to say yes to try a tool like this? Who pays for training tools today, and from which budget?
10. Would your IT or security team allow a camera at a workstation? What about video going to the cloud, versus staying on site?

**Demo and close (5 min)**
- Show the 90-second film, or the live "try it yourself" link.
- "If this worked on one of your procedures, would you try it for 6 weeks?" Write down the exact answer.
- "Who else should I talk to?"

**After each call**, write down: their role, plant size, their top pain in their own words, current tools, the budget owner, and whether they'd pilot (yes / maybe / no).

## 2. Pilot proposal (one page, send after a good call)

**Ghost Expert pilot: [Plant name], [procedure name]**

**The problem.** [In their words, from the call: e.g. "New maintenance techs take about 4 months before we trust them on lockout/tagout alone."]

**What we'll do (6 weeks)**
- **Week 1:** Your expert films the procedure once and talks through it. Ghost Expert drafts the steps, and your expert corrects and approves them.
- **Weeks 2–5:** [N] new or junior technicians practise under the coach on a phone or tablet. Every run produces a training record a supervisor signs off.
- **Week 6:** Review together.

**What we'll measure (agreed before we start)**
| Measure | Before (your current number) | Target |
|---|---|---|
| Time until a new hire is signed off on this procedure | [ ] | [ ] |
| Skipped or out-of-order steps caught in practice | [not measured today] | reported per run |
| Supervisor hours spent shadowing | [ ] | [ ] |

**What you provide:** one expert for about 2 hours, a mount for a phone or tablet over the work area, [N] trainees, and a supervisor to sign off records.

**What we provide:** the software, setup, and an on-site box if video must stay in the plant.

**Data:** Video stays [on site / in our cloud, deleted after the pilot]. Training records are yours.

**Safety:** Ghost Expert is a training tool. It does not replace your lockout/tagout program, supervision or sign-off.

**Price:** [$X for the pilot, credited against the first year if you continue].

## 3. Letter of intent (LOI) template

> **Not legal advice.** Have a lawyer check this before you send it. An LOI shows investors real intent, and it has to say clearly that it is non-binding.

[Company letterhead]

[Date]

To: [Your company name]

**Re: Letter of intent — Ghost Expert**

[Customer company] ("we") has seen Ghost Expert, a system that drafts training procedures from an expert's video and coaches technicians through them on camera.

1. **Intent.** If the pilot described below meets the agreed measures, we intend to deploy Ghost Expert at [site(s)] for [procedures / number of stations].
2. **Pilot.** A [6]-week pilot on [procedure], starting about [date], measuring [time to sign-off / errors caught / supervisor hours].
3. **Expected commercial terms.** About [$X per site per year / per station], to be set in a final agreement.
4. **Non-binding.** This letter is not binding on either party, except that each party keeps the other's confidential information confidential. Only a signed final agreement creates obligations.
5. **Reference.** [Customer company] [agrees / does not agree] that its name may be shown to prospective investors as a party to this letter.

Signed for [Customer company]: __________________ Name: __________ Title: __________ Date: ______

Signed for [Your company]: __________________ Name: __________ Title: __________ Date: ______
